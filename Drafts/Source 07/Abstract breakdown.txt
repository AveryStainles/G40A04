Accelerate fusion and fission research

14 teams explored science challenges using ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and other AI models.

//useful==================================================================
Applications ranged from:
- developing foundation models for fusion reactor control.
- automating Monte Carle simulations.
- material degradation.
- and designing experimental programs for advanced reactors.

//useful==================================================================
Teams employed structured workflows combining 
- Prompt engineering.
- Deep research capabilities.
- Iterative refinement to generate:
	- hypotheses.
	- prototype code. 
	- research strategies.
	
//useful==================================================================
Key findings demonstrate that LLMs excel at early-stages in: 
- Exploration.
- Literature synthesis.
- Workflow design.
- Successfully identifying research gaps 
	- and generating plausible experimental frameworks. 

//limitations==================================================================
However, significant limitations emerged:
- Novel materials designs, 
- Advanced code generation for modeling and simulation
- domain-specific details requiring expert validation. 


//relevant==================================================================
The successful outcomes resulted:
- expert-driven prompt engineering 
- treating AI as a complementary tool rather than a replacement
for physics-based methods. 

//relevant==================================================================
Conclusion
- The workshop validated AI’s potential to accelerate nuclear energy research through rapid iteration and cross-disciplinary synthesis while highlighting the need for curated nuclear-specific datasets, workflow automation, and specialized model development.
- These results provide a roadmap for integrating AI tools into nuclear science workflows, potentially reducing development cycles for safer, more efficient nuclear energy systems while maintaining rigorous scientific standards.
